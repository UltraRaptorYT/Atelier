# Courtyard Library — A Garden for the Neighborhood

Design a two-storey community library on a hypothetical neighborhood site in Bishan, Singapore. Site conditions and area limits are fictional client inputs, not a survey or planning entitlement.

SITE AND ENVELOPE
Flat 60 m east–west × 50 m north–south plot: 3,000 m². Assume a public park along the south, a local street to the west, and housing to the north. Bring pedestrians in from the southwest; keep deliveries at the northwest. Target 2,400 m² enclosed GFA across two floors, approximately 1,200 m² each; hard cap 2,500 m². Maximum covered footprint is 1,300 m². Place a 20 m × 15 m open-to-sky courtyard at the heart of the library; its 300 m² void is excluded from GFA on both levels. Provide at least 900 m² planted outdoor space, including that courtyard.

PROGRAM AND AREA BUDGET
Working allocations total 2,400 m²: children's library 250; adult reading and stacks 820; teen zone 120; community room and two seminar rooms 230; six small study rooms 48; entrance/issue desk and café 160; staff/support 152; toilets 80; circulation, stairs, lift reservation, and walls 540.
Provide 140 reading seats: 30 children's, 20 teen, 30 general, and 60 quiet adult seats. Community/seminar seating is additional and must not be counted toward these 140. Plan a 100 m² staff zone within the staff/support allocation.

HARD REQUIREMENTS
Keep children, café, and the community room near the ground-floor entrance. Place the quiet adult zone upstairs toward the northeast, separated from event traffic. Maintain a continuous, legible route around the courtyard. Reserve a lift and stairs linking both floors. Allow the community room to operate after library hours without opening the collections.

VALUE AND VIBE
Create a welcoming neighborhood living room that gradually becomes quieter as visitors move inward and upward. Aim for shaded daylight, tactile materials, garden views, and a restrained civic presence. The courtyard should help people orient themselves and pause, rather than be a decorative hole. Avoid an intimidating monument, a shopping-mall atrium, or an all-glass reading room.

SHOW AND EVALUATE
Show both floor plans, the courtyard void, a section through the garden, arrival and quiet-reading views, and an after-hours access diagram. Track reading-seat counts, area reconciliation, and active/quiet adjacencies. Improve V2 without sacrificing the central courtyard or independent community access. Daylight, acoustic, accessibility, and regulatory performance remain unverified until supported by appropriate evidence.
